# Peach Tree Memorial and 2025-2026 Weather Review

**Record created:** September 20, 2026  
**Weather period reviewed:** January 1, 2025 through April 1, 2026, inclusive

## Conclusion

The weather measured at Shelton Airport was abnormal in several ways relevant
to peach trees, but the record does not establish weather as the sole cause of
the five whole-tree losses.

Calendar year 2025 was not exceptionally hot or cold on average. The important
signal was the sequence of stresses: prolonged winter cold, repeated warm-then-
freeze cycles in spring, a dry and intermittently hot growing season, and a
warm, very wet December. This sequence strongly supports weather as an
explanation for blossom or crop loss and plausibly contributed to whole-tree
stress. It does not show a single historically extreme freeze or other event
that, by itself, explains why every peach tree died.

The durable inventory record should therefore state:

> Cause undetermined. The 2025-2026 weather record shows an unusual sequence of
> sustained winter cold, repeated spring freeze whiplash, growing-season
> drought and heat, and a warm, very wet December. These conditions likely
> stressed the trees and could explain crop injury, but the available evidence
> does not prove that weather alone caused the whole-tree losses.

## Evidence boundary and method

- The analyzed file contains daily precipitation and minimum, maximum, and
  average temperature observations for Shelton Airport, Washington
  (`USW00094227`).
- The target period was compared with matching periods from 1999 through 2024.
  Rankings use complete comparable periods where the metric required complete
  data.
- Calculated mean temperature uses the daily minimum and maximum when both are
  present.
- Eleven consecutive dates are missing for all relevant measurements:
  August 27 through September 6, 2025. Annual and April-September totals are
  therefore incomplete.
- The source weather file was supplied for this review and is not committed to
  this repository. SHA-256:
  `267FB67191486786ACE409CFF270F0ECBDDCF94520996C4AFDB7B5981DDE38CF`.
- Airport observations do not measure the orchard's exact temperature, wind,
  snow cover, soil temperature, soil moisture, irrigation, drainage, or
  flooding.

## Interpretation

### What the evidence supports

- **Blossom or crop injury:** Strongly supported. Peach blossoms become more
  vulnerable as warm weather advances their development, and the spring of
  2025 had a record number of screened warm-then-freeze nights.
- **Accumulated whole-tree stress:** Plausible. Prolonged cold was followed by
  dry spring and summer conditions, a hot spell, and concentrated winter rain.
  Multiple stresses can reduce resilience even when no single measurement is
  lethal.
- **Wet-root contribution:** Plausible only where drainage was poor or flooding
  occurred. The December rainfall establishes a wet weather signal but does not
  establish conditions around the roots.

### What the evidence does not support

- **A single unprecedented killing freeze:** Not found. The lowest measured
  temperature was within the station's historical range.
- **An exceptionally wet winter overall:** Not found. October-March
  precipitation was moderately above average, not extreme.
- **An exceptional January-March 2026 season:** Not found. Temperature and
  precipitation were close to their historical means.
- **One weather exposure shared equally by all five trees:** Not established.
  Rio Oso Gem was planted in 2024, O'Henry in 2025, and the other three in 2026.
  Without exact planting dates, the amount of target-period weather each tree
  experienced cannot be determined.

## Remaining unknowns

- Exact planting, symptom-onset, and death dates for each tree
- Rootstock, tree age at planting, and pre-plant storage conditions
- Orchard-level minimum temperatures and snow cover
- Soil type, drainage, standing-water duration, irrigation, and root-zone
  moisture
- Root or crown disease, cankers, animal girdling, herbicide exposure, and
  transplant injury

## Recommended record

Retain the cause of death as **undetermined** and record the unusual weather
sequence as a plausible contributing factor. Before replanting peaches in the
same locations, inspect drainage and preserved root/crown tissue if available.
Photographs or samples showing the root collar, cambium, graft union, roots,
gumming, cankers, or girdling would materially improve diagnosis.

## Obituaries

### Frost Peach (Inventory 007)

Frost Peach came to the farm from Trees of Antiquity and was planted in 2026.
A self-fertile California variety dating to the 1960s, it was selected for its
late bloom, midseason harvest, and fruit suited to fresh eating, canning, and
pies.

Its time in the orchard was brief, but the hope represented by its planting was
not. Frost is remembered as one of five peach trees whose loss led to a closer
study of the farm's weather and growing conditions. Its exact date and cause of
death remain unknown.

### Baby Crawford Peach (Inventory 035)

Baby Crawford Peach came to the farm from Trees of Antiquity and was planted in
2026. This self-fertile heirloom traces its history to Ohio in the 1870s and
promised a midseason harvest for eating fresh, canning, and pies.

Baby Crawford did not remain long enough to fulfill that promise. Its place in
the inventory now preserves both the tree and the intention behind planting an
old American peach for a new generation. Its exact date and cause of death
remain unknown.

### George IV Peach (Inventory 047)

George IV Peach came to the farm from Trees of Antiquity and was planted in
2026. Originating in New York in the 1820s, this self-fertile heirloom carried
two centuries of orchard history and the promise of an early peach harvest.

Its chapter on the farm was short. George IV is remembered for the history it
brought to the orchard and for the harvest that was imagined when it was
planted. Its exact date and cause of death remain unknown.

### Rio Oso Gem Peach (Inventory 071)

Rio Oso Gem Peach came to the farm from Trees of Antiquity and was planted in
2024. A self-fertile California variety introduced in 1926, it promised a late
harvest of peaches for fresh eating, canning, and pies.

As the earliest-planted peach in this memorial, Rio Oso Gem held the longest
recorded place in the orchard. Its loss closes the first chapter of peach
growing documented in this inventory, but its name and history remain part of
the farm record. Its exact date and cause of death remain unknown.

### O'Henry Peach (Inventory 080)

O'Henry Peach came to the farm from Trees of Antiquity and was planted in 2025.
This self-fertile California variety from the 1950s was chosen for a late
harvest and fruit suited to fresh eating, canning, and pies.

O'Henry's time on the farm spanned part of the unusual 2025-2026 weather period.
The record cannot say which conditions determined its fate, but it preserves
the tree as more than a missing inventory row: it was a deliberate planting
with a season and harvest in mind. Its exact date and cause of death remain
unknown.
